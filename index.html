Questions

How long does it really last?

Rice, wheat grain, sugar and salt are built for 20 years or more. Dals, oats and milk powder for 10 to 20. Oil, ghee and atta for 1 to 2. Every tin shows its own date, set by when it still tastes right, not just when it's safe.

How much do I need?

Plan on about 2,000 kcal per person per day. For a family of 4, a month of staples is roughly 30 kg rice, 20 kg wheat, 12 kg dal, 2 kg sugar and some salt, plus oil. The planner works it out for you.

How much space does it take?

A month for a family of 4 is around 28 tins, about two cupboard shelves. The tins stack, so a loft or the top of a cupboard works.

Once I open a tin, how long does it keep?

Snap the lid back on and use it like any rice or dal in your kitchen, within a few weeks to months. Unopened tins are the ones that keep for years.

Where should I store it?

The coolest, driest spot you have, off the floor and out of the sun. Heat shortens shelf life for any food, so a storeroom or inside cupboard is better than a hot loft.

What if there's no gas?

The no-gas meals cook in the pouch with hot water, or with ordinary water if you wait longer. Oats and poha also need very little fuel.

How is this different from rice from the shop?

Shop packets let air and moisture in, so rice gets weevils and dal goes stale within months. Ours is dried, sealed with the oxygen removed and packed in metal that keeps out light, damp and pests.

Why wheat grain and not atta?

Whole wheat atta goes stale in a year or two because of its natural oils. Wheat grain keeps for decades, and any local chakki will mill it in minutes.

Do I need to store water too?

Yes, for emergencies. We don't sell water, but keep about 4 litres per person for each day you might be without supply.
